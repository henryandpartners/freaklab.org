02The Question

What role does life science play in protecting, restoring — or reinventing — nature?

Technology is constantly redefining our understanding of life, bringing biology to a pivotal turning point where humans and machines collaborate to engineer new forms of existence.

Intimacy with Nature invites us to reflect on our interconnected relationship with the natural world, animals, and technology — to recall forgotten lessons, and consider which mistakes we must not repeat.

03Who We Are

FREAK Lab

The Futuristic Research Cluster — an anti-disciplinary research cluster exploring the symbiotic relationship between human, art and technology.

Founded in 2017 and based at King Mongkut's University of Technology Thonburi (KMUTT), FREAK Lab prototypes and researches while leveraging art and installations to reimagine possible realities — bringing scholars, artists and technicians together to transform ideas of the future into practical, feasible applications.

It travels across continents to find new encounters in different natural environments.

Going inside the laboratory

Naresuan University · Phitsanulok, Thailand

Co-hosted by Prof. Pakpoom Subsoontorn and the Bangkok International Performing Arts Meeting (BIPAM), the first iteration of Intimacy with Nature brought seven artists in residence into a working research university — with a one-week public workshop alongside.

Phase 1 · Thailand 202205 / 21
06Phase 1 · In the Lab

Learning the instruments of life

Participants were introduced to synthetic biology and learnt to extract DNA with their own hands — then trained on PCR and gel electrophoresis, the everyday tools of molecular biology.

Alongside bench work, they met faculty members and researchers from the Medical Science and Agricultural departments — science as conversation, not lecture.

What was learned: the materiality of biotechnology — that DNA is a substance you can hold, amplify, and read.

08Phase 1 · Residue

Slime mold, artists, and seven new questions

Artist Hanna Saito led a slime-mold workshop — working with an organism that thinks without a brain, and remaps how we imagine intelligence and growth.

Seven artists in residence left the week with laboratory literacy: extraction, cultivation, observation — and a shared vocabulary between art and science.

  • Synthetic biology demystified — hands on real lab protocols
  • Local agricultural science as a living context for biotech
  • Art + science as one studio practice, not two fields

14The Thread

From bench to garden to city — a network forms

Phase 1 gave artists laboratory literacy. Phase 2 opened the laboratory to the city — and showed that labs already exist in gardens, farms and kitchens.

Across the two phases, one community formed: biologists, artists, curators and makers from Thailand, Singapore, Japan, Indonesia, Taiwan, Korea and beyond — sharing the principle of the Global Community Bio Summit: democratize biotechnology, build an inclusive commons of knowledge.

The question that remains: what should this network become? Where does the conversation go next?

21Beyond the Symposium · Open Call

Art Residency at the Botanical Garden Network, Thailand

How does local ecology adapt to changing environments — shifting climate, industrial expansion, the pressure of growth?

This residency invites artists and researchers to work across Thailand's botanical garden network: to explore the pace of change, and to revisit the idea — and the ideal — of nature.

  • Fieldwork inside living collections across Thailand
  • Artists and researchers in shared residence
  • Revisiting what "nature" means under anthropogenic change
